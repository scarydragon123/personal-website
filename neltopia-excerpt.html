Chapter One: The Vanishing

T

he day everything changed started like any other. Ella was walking home from school, taking her usual shortcut through Palmer Park, when the first shadow appeared. It wasn't the normal kind of shadow—the type cast by trees or clouds or the occasional passing bird. This shadow moved against the wind, twisting and writhing like smoke underwater.

She stopped, her worn sneakers scuffing against the gravel path. The autumn air, which moments ago had been crisp and inviting, suddenly felt thick with anticipation. The shadow danced closer, and Ella could have sworn she heard it whisper her name.

"Hello?" she called out, immediately regretting the tremor in her voice. The shadow paused, hovering just at the edge of her vision. Then, like ink dropped into clear water, it began to spread and take shape.

That's when she heard the scream.

It came from somewhere behind the old oak tree—the one all the kids said was haunted because of its twisted branches and the strange symbols that sometimes appeared in its bark. Ella knew she should run. Every instinct told her to turn around and sprint back toward the safety of the school.

But the scream had sounded like Axel.

And Axel was her best friend.

Heart pounding against her ribs like a trapped bird, Ella took one step forward. The shadow seemed to pulse with excitement. Another step. The air grew colder, and the whispers grew louder, though she still couldn't make out what they were saying.

As she rounded the oak tree, the world seemed to hold its breath. There, suspended in a cocoon of writhing shadows, was Axel. His eyes were wide with terror, his mouth open in a silent scream. And standing before him, tall and terrible in a coat that seemed to be made of living darkness, was a figure Ella would come to know as Master Loss.

The figure turned, and though Ella couldn't see a face beneath its hood, she felt its gaze pierce through her like a winter wind. "Ah," it said, in a voice that sounded like rustling leaves and creaking branches. "The final piece arrives."

Before Ella could move, before she could even draw breath to scream, the shadows surged forward. They wrapped around her like cold silk, and the world began to fade. The last thing she saw was a young girl—Isabella from her math class—stepping out from behind another tree, her own form already half-consumed by the encroaching darkness.

Then everything went black, and Ella felt herself falling into a world where magic was real, shadows had teeth, and nothing would ever be the same again.
